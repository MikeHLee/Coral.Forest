==============================================================================

CHECK OF THE COLUMN SUM AGAINST THE TOTAL THAT THE TABLE PRINTS
==============================================================================

TEST 1. Sum of num_reef_fishers against the printed Total row.
  Sum of the 98 values in the CSV file = 6122041 reef fishers
  The source prints a Total row at raw/teh_S1.txt lines 994 to 996:
      line 994: Total
      line 996:            6,145,200
  Difference: 6145200 minus 6122041 = 23159 reef fishers.
  Result: NOT consistent. The CSV sum is short by 23159 reef fishers, which is
  0.38% of the printed total.

DIAGNOSIS OF THE DIFFERENCE

  Step 1. The paper states that the final calculation covers 99 countries.
  Article text, verbatim: 'The final number of coral reef fishers in 99
  countries (excluding Singapore) was then calculated as the average of
  approach 1 ... and 2'. Source: the PLOS article XML at
  https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0065397&type=manuscript

  Step 2. The published Table S3 holds only 98 country rows. The analyst
  counted the rows in two independent ways and got 98 both times:
    - textutil -convert txt, then a parse anchored on the 5 region names: 98
    - textutil -convert html, then a parse of the HTML <tr> elements: 98
  Both parses list every non-empty line in the table. No line is unused. So no
  row was lost in the conversion. The row is absent from the published file.

  Step 3. The analyst located the region of the missing row. Table 4 of the
  article gives the regional totals in millions. The CSV regional sums are:
      Eastern Pacific/Atlantic   35 countries     480545 fishers  = 0.48 million
      Indian Ocean               14 countries    1502590 fishers  = 1.50 million
      Middle East/NE Africa      14 countries     344549 fishers  = 0.34 million
      Southeast Asia             12 countries    3348588 fishers  = 3.35 million
      Western Pacific            23 countries     445769 fishers  = 0.45 million
    Article Table 4 prints:
      Southeast Asia             3.35 million   -> CSV gives 3.35  MATCH
      Indian Ocean               1.50 million   -> CSV gives 1.50  MATCH
      E. Pacific/Atlantic        0.50 million   -> CSV gives 0.48  MISMATCH
      Western Pacific            0.45 million   -> CSV gives 0.45  MATCH
      Middle East                0.34 million   -> CSV gives 0.34  MATCH
      TOTAL                      6.14 million
    Adding the missing 23,159 fishers to the Eastern Pacific/Atlantic sum gives
    480,545 + 23,159 = 503,704, which rounds to 0.50 million and matches Table 4.

  Step 4. Conclusion. One country in the Eastern Pacific/Atlantic region, with
  23,159 reef fishers, is missing from the published Table S3. The analyst
  could NOT identify which country it is. The analyst compared the Table S3
  country list with Table 2 of the article (97 countries) and with Table S2 of
  the supplement. Every country in those lists is already present in Table S3.
  The analyst did NOT add a row for the missing country and did NOT alter any
  value. The CSV file holds exactly the 98 rows that the source prints.

==============================================================================
MISSING COLUMNS
==============================================================================

Table S3 holds NO population column and NO reef-area column. The four printed
columns are: Country, Region, No. of reef fishers, and Year and source of
alternate estimate. The task asked for a population or reef-area column. The
analyst did not add one, because the source table has none.

Related data that the paper puts in OTHER tables, not in Table S3:
  - Article Table 2 gives a rural coastal population LEVEL per country as the
    words Low, Medium or High. Article Table 2 gives no population count.
  - Article Table 3 gives a fisher density in fishers per square kilometre of
    fishing ground, by population level and fishing pressure level. Article
    Table 3 gives no per-country reef area.
