CHECK OF THE COLUMN SUMS AGAINST THE TOTALS THAT THE PAPER STATES
==============================================================================

The paper does not print a global total inside any of these three tables.
The paper prints global totals in the running text. The tables hold only the
top 15 or the top 30 countries. Therefore each column sum must be SMALLER than
the global total. The analyst used this as the consistency test.

TEST 1. Annual averted damages.
  Sum of the 15 values in annual_averted_damages_musd = 3099 million US dollars
  Paper text (raw/beck_main_layout.txt line 159): 'reefs reduce the annual
  expected damages from storms by more than $4 billion'.
  4000 million minus 3099 million = 901 million.
  Result: consistent. The top 15 countries hold about 77% of the global total.

TEST 2. Annual avoided people flooded.
  Sum of the 30 values in annual_avoided_people_flooded = 197797 people per year
  Paper text (raw/beck_main_layout.txt lines 201 to 202): 'Reefs also reduce
  annual flooding by more than 200,000 people (Fig. 2)'.
  200000 minus 197797 = 2203 people.
  Result: consistent. The top 30 countries hold about 98.9% of the global total.

TEST 2b. Cross-check of the people total by a second route.
  Paper text (raw/beck_main_layout.txt lines 112 to 113): 'the flooding of land
  would increase by 69% and ... affecting 81% more people annually (Fig. 2)'.
  Let Y be the global annual people flooded with reefs. Then 1.81*Y - Y = the
  avoided people, which the paper states is more than 200,000.
  0.81*Y = 200000 gives Y = 246,914 and 1.81*Y = 446,914.
  Both values fit the axis range of Figure 2 (0 to 700 thousand people).
  Result: the two statements in the paper agree with each other, and the CSV
  sum of 197797 people is 98.9% of the 200,000 global figure.

TEST 3. Annual avoided area flooded.
  Sum of the 30 values in annual_avoided_area_flooded_km2 = 451.79 square kilometres
  Result: NOT verifiable against a printed number. The paper gives the global
  land-area result only as a percentage change ('increase by 69%') and as a bar
  chart (Figure 2). The paper prints no absolute global land-area value in the
  text. The analyst therefore cannot compute a global total to compare with.
  The analyst did confirm that the 30 values fall in rank order from 88.45 down
  to 0.50 square kilometres, with no break in the order.

TEST 4. Averted built capital for the 100-year event.
  Sum of the 15 values in averted_built_capital_100yr_busd = 169.90 billion US dollars
  Paper text (raw/beck_main_layout.txt lines 120 to 122): 'For 100-year events,
  the topmost 1 m of reefs provide flood reduction benefits that result in $130
  billion in avoided damages'.
  Result: NOT consistent as a direct sum. The $130 billion figure is avoided
  DAMAGE to built capital. Supplementary Table 1 reports avoided FLOODING of
  built capital, which is the exposed asset value, not the damaged value. The
  paper applies a damage function to convert exposure into damage. The exposure
  sum (169.90 billion) is larger than the damage figure (130 billion), which is
  the expected direction. The analyst could NOT verify this column against a
  printed total.

==============================================================================
VALUES THAT THE ANALYST COULD NOT VERIFY
==============================================================================

1. The unit of the column annual_averted_damages_over_gdp.
   The table header prints 'Annual averted damages/GDP' with no unit and no
   percent sign. The analyst read the same header in the publisher HTML table at
   https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-04568-z/tables/1 . The header is
   identical there. The magnitude of the values matches a percent, not a plain
   ratio. Example: Cuba has 401 million US dollars of averted damage and a 2011
   gross domestic product near 69 billion US dollars. 401/69000 = 0.58 percent.
   The table prints 0.25. The order of magnitude fits percent. The analyst
   reports the value as printed and does NOT assert the unit.

2. There is no country table for the 10-year, 25-year and 50-year storm events.
   Supplementary Table 1 covers the 100-year event only. The analyst searched
   both PDF files for the strings 'return period', '10-year', '25-year',
   '50-year' and '100-year'. Every other result for a storm return period is a
   global figure (Figure 3 and Figure 4 of the main paper) or a methods
   sentence. Supplementary Data 1 (file 41467_2018_4568_MOESM4_ESM.xlsx) holds a
   methods comparison table only, with no country data.
