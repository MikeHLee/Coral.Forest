==============================================================================
CHECK OF THE COLUMN SUMS AGAINST THE TOTAL THAT THE PAPER STATES
==============================================================================

TEST 1. Sum of reef-associated visitor expenditure.
  Sum of the 80 values = 35851038 thousand US dollars
  This equals 35.851 billion US dollars.
  The paper abstract states, verbatim: 'Some 30% of the world's reefs are of
  value in the tourism sector, with a total value estimated at nearly US$36
  billion, or over 9% of all coastal tourism value in the world's coral reef
  countries.'
  Source of the abstract text: the OpenAlex record for DOI
  10.1016/j.marpol.2017.05.014, read on 2026-09-19. The ScienceDirect article
  page returned HTTP 403 and the analyst could not read the article PDF.
  Difference: 35.851 billion minus 36 billion = -0.149 billion US dollars.
  Result: consistent. The CSV sum is 35.851 billion, which the abstract describes
  as 'nearly US$36 billion'. The remaining gap is the 22 jurisdictions that
  Table A1 leaves out. Table A1 covers only the 80 places with more than 50 square kilometres
  of reef and more than 10 million US dollars of reef-related spending. The
  paper reports tourism data for 102 countries and territories in total
  (raw/sp_layout.txt, section A3.1).

TEST 2. Sum of all visitor spending.
  Sum of the 80 values = 2300063394 thousand US dollars = 2300.1 billion US dollars.
  Result: NOT verifiable. The paper prints no global total for this column.

TEST 3. Row count.
  The CSV file holds 80 rows.
  The table caption states 'N=80'.
  Result: consistent. The counts match exactly.

==============================================================================
VALUES THAT THE ANALYST COULD NOT VERIFY, AND MISSING COLUMNS
==============================================================================

1. Table A1 holds NO reef-area column. The task asked for a reef-area column.
   The table gives 'Mean value of reef (US$ km-2)' instead. A reader can derive
   an implied reef area by dividing column 5 by column 8, but the paper does
   not print the reef area, so the analyst did not add a derived column.

2. The table prints 'n/a' in some cells. The CSV file leaves those cells empty.
   Counts of empty cells:
     reef_visitor_expenditure_pct_of_total_tourism        1 empty cells
     reef_tourism_pct_of_gdp                              19 empty cells
     mean_value_of_reef_usd_per_km2                       3 empty cells

3. The column reef_visitor_expenditure_pct_of_total_tourism is empty for
   Northern Mariana Islands, because the source prints '$0' for all visitor
   spending and 'n/a' for the proportion. The analyst kept the printed values.
